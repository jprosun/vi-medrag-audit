\begin{abstract}
Retrieval-augmented generation is adopted for medical question answering on a promise of
traceability, yet whether a citation opens is rarely checked, and whether the cited text
answers the question less often still. We audit a deployed Vietnamese corpus and its serving
path. Of \num{595663} indexed chunks, \SI{43.1}{\percent} carry no source URL and
\SI{13.4}{\percent} only an issue-level one---and those \num{79678} chunks share just
\num{143} distinct destinations, \num{142} of which resolve to an index that does not identify
the cited article. The uncitable stratum supplies \SI{61.4}{\percent} of what retrieval
returns. Withholding uncitable claims after generation retains \num{0.527} of them and leaves
79 of 134 precommitted questions with none, varying \num{5.6}-fold by question type.
Excluding the stratum at retrieval instead eliminates citability-based withholding by
construction: retention \num{0.960} and 25 answers with no attribution claim. We found no
evidence that either design changed the per-claim rates---under both, about \num{0.96} of
returned claims are supported by their cited passage and \numrange{0.02}{0.04} answer the
question, depending on the judge---and no equivalence margin was pre-specified. Support and responsiveness are not substitutes.
\end{abstract}

% ---------------------------------------------------------------- I. INTRODUCTION (<=450 words)
\section{Introduction}Retrieval-augmented generation is deployed for medical question answering on a promise: the
answer is grounded in retrieved documents, and a reader who doubts it can check. In the
Vietnamese-language system we operate, that promise failed quietly. Over 134 questions,
\num{156} of the \num{351} claims the system proposed were uncitable---\num{154} citing a
record that carries no source URL and two naming no record at all---counted over every claim
the extractor produced, before any gate. In the 154 URL-less cases, the cited records exist
but lack source links: their corpus was re-packaged for embedding training with its text
kept and its URLs dropped. No answer-quality metric we had could see this.

The failure separates two things easily conflated. A citation emitted by a language model is
a promise; whether it can be opened is a property, and only code holds a property. Our own
earlier verifier checked only that a citation index fell within the range of retrieved
chunks---a test any well-formed citation to a URL-less record passes.

Mechanisms that make citations verifiable by construction are established and we claim
novelty for none of them (Section~\ref{sec:related}); what their evaluations share is a
setting, English corpora assembled so that they can be cited. Healthcare deployment outside
English meets the opposite condition---a corpus assembled to be retrieved, for a purpose that
was not citation---and what an enforcement instrument meets there is unreported.

We therefore treat the mechanism as an instrument, not a contribution, and ask three
questions. \textbf{RQ1:} how much of a deployed Vietnamese medical corpus can support a
citation a reader can actually open---a locator stored, answering, and resolving to the cited
article rather than to an index? \textbf{RQ2:} what do span containment and stored-URL
citability cost at serving time, and how does that cost depend on where filtering is applied?
\textbf{RQ3:} does what survives enforcement
answer the question that was asked?

Our contribution is empirical: retrieval \emph{amplifies} provenance loss, and where the
citability check sits decides the cost. Around it we separate three routinely conflated
quantities---whether a source can be traced, whether the passage supports the claim, and
whether the claim answers the question---end to end on a deployed system.
(i) An exhaustive census of all \num{595663} indexed chunks in which the untraceable stratum
supplies a larger share of retrieved chunks than of the index, plus a dereference audit
showing a stored locator is a weak proxy for an openable one: \num{142} of the \num{143}
issue-level destinations resolve to an index rather than to the cited article.
(ii) A price for each check, from four nested gate configurations replayed over one captured
sweep of 134 questions hashed and published before any of them ran, so they share identical
generations; the gap they leave is that \SI{77.8}{\percent} of the figures in delivered
answers are pinned to nothing. (iii) The same questions run live under a second design that
excludes the URL-less stratum at retrieval instead. (iv) Two judgement instruments, each
frozen before use and each with a control arm. Evaluation code, questions, labels, judging
prompts and redacted run records are released at
\url{https://github.com/jprosun/vi-medrag-audit}.
Source passages are withheld; text-dependent verification requires the original corpus.
% ---------------------------------------------------------------- II. RELATED WORK (350--500 words; do NOT cut first)
\section{Related Work}\label{sec:related}
\textbf{A citation is a promise, not a property.} Generative search engines produce citations
that often fail to support the sentence they are attached to \cite{liu2023verifiability};
hence benchmarks for citation quality \cite{gao2023alce} and automatic attribution scoring
\cite{yue2023attrscore}. An audit of deep-research agents finds link validity above
\SI{94}{\percent} alongside factual accuracy between \SI{39}{\percent} and \SI{77}{\percent}
\cite{onweller2026citednotverified}: a citation that resolves is not one that supports. Nor is a
supported claim a responsive one: answer coverage has been scored by decomposing the query
into sub-questions \cite{xie2025subquestion}, where we judge each claim against the whole
question instead.

\textbf{Verifiable by construction.} Requiring each statement to carry a verbatim quote is
established. GopherCite abstains unless it can produce one \cite{menick2022gophercite}; later
work prompts models to quote pre-training data \cite{weller2024according}, aligns them to
\cite{zhang2024quotetuning}, locates spans post hoc
\cite{yeginbergen2026fullcite}, and verifies each claim before release
\cite{qian2025vericite}. In medicine, claim-selective certification reports zero
unsupported-critical-claim risk under a weak-label certificate protocol
\cite{kan2026claimselective}, and a buffer holding
streamed tokens until a guard clears them has been proposed \cite{sentguard2026}. Ours are the
simplest published forms of these, used as an instrument rather than proposed as a method.

\textbf{What a guarantee costs.} That enforcing correctness withholds content is known in the
abstract: conformal methods trade coverage for a factuality guarantee \cite{mohri2024conformal},
and citation correctness trades against coverage across paradigms \cite{saxena2025coverage}.
Both are established offline, on English benchmarks, against corpora assembled to be cited.
What such an instrument meets on a serving path over a corpus never assembled that way is
unreported.

\textbf{Medical and Vietnamese resources.} Medical RAG is benchmarked for answer quality
\cite{xiong2024mirage}, and Vietnamese medical NLP has produced question-answering datasets
\cite{tran2024vimedaqa}, benchmarks \cite{vm14k2025} and graph-grounded systems for healthcare
regulation \cite{vihermes2026}. To our knowledge none audits a Vietnamese medical retrieval
corpus for whether its records can be cited at all. How a corpus loses that property is itself
documented: an audit of over 1{,}800 text datasets found licences omitted for more than
\SI{70}{\percent} and mis-stated for more than \SI{50}{\percent} on popular platforms
\cite{longpre2024provenance}. We measure what that loss costs at serving time.

\section{System and Enforcement as Instrument}\subsection{Definitions}
The system answers Vietnamese medical questions over a Qdrant index of \num{595663} chunks
embedded with \texttt{bge-m3}, combining dense retrieval with an article-level lexical
index. Its evidence extractor emits, per proposed claim, a triple: the \emph{claim}, a
\emph{supporting span}, and the \emph{chunk} the span is drawn from. Every check below is
defined on that triple.

The configuration is mixed and chosen per question by a router: of the 134, \num{67} ran a
\emph{standard} profile with the LLM extractor unused and \num{67} a \emph{deep} profile with
it engaged, identically per question in both arms, so this is not a between-arm confound. The
no-extractor half produced the \emph{majority} of claims, \num{208} of \num{351}. Its recorded
top-$k$, \num{12} against \num{20}, sets a base pool rather than a delivered count---the served
pool is that base times a mode multiplier---so the answers carry \numrange{22}{57} retrieved
chunks each, \num{4024} in all.

A span is \emph{located} when it occurs as an exact substring of the chunk it names, after a
normalisation the released code documents in full: NFC, header removal, lowercasing,
unification of Vietnamese digit-group and decimal separators (so $96{,}18$ and $96.18$
compare equal), dash and quote variants, whitespace. Diacritics are deliberately \emph{not}
folded---two Vietnamese words can differ by tone alone---and spans under 12 characters are
rejected, since a very short span matches almost anything.

A record is \emph{citable} when it carries a non-empty source URL. We report the URL at three
granularities---article, issue, none---because a citation landing on a journal issue rather
than on the article is a weaker guarantee than the binary suggests (Table~\ref{tab:census}).

A \emph{claim figure} is a percentage, a decimal, or an integer of at least three digits,
citation markers removed and bare four-digit values in 1900--2099 excluded as years unless
digit-grouped. A figure is \emph{evidence-absent} when it appears in no located span \emph{and} nowhere in
the retrieved evidence---lexical absence, not a finding that the value is wrong---and
\emph{unattributed} when it appears in that evidence but inside no located span. Reporting
the two as one hides the one that dominates.

\subsection{Gates}
Four checks run in code, in this order, before the answer is verified. Every withheld claim
records a \texttt{drop\_reason}, which is what makes the offline replay of
Table~\ref{tab:conditions} possible.

\textbf{Span gate.} Each claim's span is tested for containment in the chunk it cites. A claim that fails is withheld, with the reason recorded and returned: no span, an unresolvable chunk identifier, or a span not found in the cited chunk.

\textbf{Citability gate.} A claim whose span was located but whose record carries no source
URL is withheld. The chunk still reaches the generator as context; it may no longer carry a
citation. Located-ness and returned-ness are counted separately, so enforcing citability does
not deflate the span measurement.

\textbf{Numeric gate.} Statements carrying an evidence-absent figure are removed and a visible note
says what went and why; unattributed figures are counted and left. Enforcement is mechanical
because instruction was measured not to work: a prompt already forbidding invented figures was
violated, and strengthening its wording did not reduce the count.

\textbf{Verifier, fail-closed.} The mechanical findings go \emph{inside} the verifier's
prompt rather than being merged into its output afterwards, and it may not return
\texttt{pass} while any of them stands. Every path that could return without a clearance fails
closed---no client, an exception, an unusable status, or a \texttt{pass} over a standing
finding, which is downgraded to \texttt{revise}.

Failing closed has a measured cost: a truncated reply carries no status, which is correctly
not read as approval and refused 8 of 30 pilot answers. Such a reply now recovers its verdict
from the prefix before failing closed, and a \texttt{pass} so recovered is downgraded.

\textbf{Buffer-then-commit.} Tokens are held until verification returns: a block verdict can
replace the payload but cannot un-send what a reader has seen. With the guard off, a control
arm leaked \num{2326}--\num{3166} token events before the verdict, and on 2 of 8 queries the
streamed text differed from the final answer.

\subsection{What the guarantee is and is not}
Span containment proves that the cited string \emph{exists} in the cited chunk. It does not prove that the string \emph{supports} the claim; entailment is a separate measurement, reported in Section~\ref{sec:cost}. Consequently the rate of returned claims whose span verifies is 1.00 \emph{by
construction}---the response is built from the claims that passed---and we state it once as
a design invariant, not a result. It is also nearly vacuous: \num{183} of the \num{185}
returned claims have a span identical to the claim, the extractor selecting a chunk sentence
as both, so containment reduces to string identity on all but two.
Section~\ref{sec:cost} reports the quantities that can fail.

Two scope statements follow. ``Citation'' means an entry of the machine-readable attribution
list returned with each answer, not a context chunk shown as background; since the interface
does not yet render attribution cards, we say a claim is \emph{returned} rather than
displayed, and every count below is over API responses. And the narrative answer runs under a
hybrid policy permitting uncited background knowledge: enforcement covers figures, citations
and the evidence layer, not every sentence of prose.

\section{Corpus Attributability Audit}\label{sec:audit}
RQ1 asks how much of this corpus can support a citation a reader can open. We answer it by
counting, not sampling: one payload-only scroll of the live collection, all
\num{595663} points, refusing to report if the scrolled total disagrees with the
collection count (Table~\ref{tab:census}).

\textbf{Two fifths of the index cannot be cited at all.} \num{256415} chunks,
\SI{43.05}{\percent}, carry no source URL. They are a single stratum: a Hugging Face
re-packaging of a pharmacy chain's patient-education pages, ingested with its text and
without its links. The chunks are topically relevant and are still retrieved as context;
what they cannot do is carry a citation. We name the mechanism rather than the dataset
because it generalises: a public corpus re-packaged for embedding training preserves what a
retriever scores and discards what a reader would follow. Recovering the URLs is future
work.

\textbf{The untraceable stratum wins retrieval.} Its share of what is retrieved exceeds its share of the index: over the 134 reported
questions it supplied \num{2472} of \num{4024} retrieved chunks, \SI{61.4}{\percent},
against \SI{43.1}{\percent} of the corpus, while English reference material fell from
\SI{16.7}{\percent} of the index to \SI{0.3}{\percent} of what was retrieved. Every
retrieved chunk lacking a source URL came from it, and it supplied \num{154} of the
\num{156} uncitable claims. The other two cite \emph{nothing}: their chunk identifier is the
empty string, so they name neither a record nor a URL, and the span gate withheld them for
that. This is what joins the two halves of this paper: the corpus is not merely part-untraceable, the untraceable part is what the retriever prefers, so the audit's shares understate what enforcement meets at serving time.

\textbf{A binary citability test hides a second loss.} The gate asks only whether a source
URL is non-empty. Classifying the stored URL at three granularities shows that a further
\num{79678} chunks, \SI{13.4}{\percent}, carry an \emph{issue}-level URL rather than one
naming the cited article, from exactly four of the five Vietnamese journal strata; the
largest journal is entirely article-level. A reader following such a citation lands on a
table of contents and must find the sentence themselves.

\textbf{A stored locator is not one a reader can open.}
Every figure above describes the stored \emph{field}, so we dereferenced the URLs themselves,
honouring \texttt{robots.txt}---which refused five hosts covering \num{24209} chunks.

One result needs no sampling. The \num{143} distinct issue-level URLs are a \emph{census}:
\num{142} answer and land on an issue index, which does not uniquely identify the cited
article. They stand behind \num{79678} chunks, about \num{557} each, so Table~\ref{tab:census}'s
\SI{13.4}{\percent} issue stratum is not a long tail of weak citations but a handful of tables
of contents doing duty for an eighth of the corpus.

In total we probed \num{367} URLs: \num{289} frozen before any request, including the census,
and \num{78} added adaptively, hence exploratory. Failures clustered by host:
\texttt{rarediseases.info.nih.gov} answered 404 on
\num{19} of \num{19} probes and two smaller NIH hosts on all \num{16} of theirs---every URL
sampled on three hosts holding \num{2880} URLs behind \num{16045} chunks. Chunk-weighted reachability is \num{0.95},
with a joint interval across hosts of \numrange{0.72}{0.97}.

We report no corpus-level composite: it would rest on a weighted estimator this sample cannot
stabilise, which computed unbiasedly returns a rate above one. The destination test itself
held up---an author labelled \num{40} reached pages blind and it agreed on \num{39}, with no
page turning out to be a homepage, a search page or a soft \num{404}---but a confirmatory
probability-proportional sample of never-probed URLs puts chunk-weighted reachability at
\num{0.93} on that domain, with no matching interval this design supports, so the census,
not a composite, is what this
audit supports.

\textbf{Attributability is capped upstream of any gate.} The PDF extractor interleaved
journal columns, so a span can be locatable yet unquotable---damage documented at benchmark
level, reading order worst of all \cite{ocrhinders2025}. Span-verified extraction of a sample
size or primary numeric finding stood at 0.573 on 150 articles and 0.933 after repair, sample
and prompt held. Duplication compounded it \cite{lee2022dedup}: \SI{37.4}{\percent} of the
journal stratum were exact duplicates, one identity per galley file, and deduplication
replaced \num{152275} points with \num{132225}.

\textbf{The corpus has no usable time axis.} \num{636} chunks, \SI{0.1}{\percent}, carry a
publication date, all from one English patient-information stratum and \emph{none} from a
Vietnamese one. This metadata cannot establish evidence recency; the probe set therefore
treats recency questions as abstention controls, not a demonstrated absence of temporal
information in the full text.

\textbf{Where citation is possible, extraction is reliable.} On a stratified sample of 150 tier-A
articles, span-verified extraction of a study's sample size, primary numeric finding,
population, design and outcome succeeds at \numrange{0.938}{0.959} across the 145 Vietnamese
journal articles. The five English reference articles yield \emph{no}
verified field under the same Vietnamese-oriented prompt---a property of the extractor---so
we report the strata apart rather than the pooled \numrange{0.907}{0.927}. These values are
\textbf{model-extracted and span-verified}; the human precision check specified for this
audit was never performed.

% ---------------------------------------------------------------- V. ATTRIBUTION GAP AND COST OF ENFORCEMENT (<=700 words, Tables III--IV)
\section{Attribution Gap and the Cost of Enforcement}\label{sec:cost}
All results below are on 134 questions written to a specialty $\times$ question-type grid,
hashed and committed \emph{before} any condition ran, and published with this paper. The
four gate configurations are replayed offline from one captured sweep, so they share
identical generations and no cross-configuration sampling noise exists. They are
configurations of one serving path, not four systems: Section~\ref{sec:where} compares two
designs that were each run end to end.

\subsection{What enforcement changes for the reader}
Without any check, the system emits claims a reader cannot follow and figures that are not
in the evidence. Of 351 extracted claims, \num{156} are uncitable---\num{154} citing a
record with no source URL, two citing no record at all; \num{67} figures appear in no
retrieved evidence at all, in a quarter of answers
(\num{0.254}, 95\,\% CI [\num{0.187}, \num{0.328}]). Enforcement withholds every citation to a
URL-less record by construction and removes the 58 statements that carried an
evidence-absent figure; no delivered answer retains one.

The gap it does \emph{not} close is larger than the one it does. Of \num{708} figures in the
delivered answers, \num{157}---\SI{22.2}{\percent}---appear among a verified span's numbers,
membership by numeric token and so no evidence of the quantity or unit meant. The
remaining \num{551}, \SI{77.8}{\percent}, are present in the retrieved evidence but pinned
to nothing.
A system can satisfy ``every returned claim is span-verified'' and still deliver most of
its quantities unattributed, which no single ``numbers grounded'' flag can show.

\subsection{Does the citation support the claim?}
Containment is not support, so we measured support. Two independent \emph{model} judges
labelled all \num{185} returned claims against the passage each cites---blind to each other
and to the system's verdicts, under a codebook frozen beforehand, with \num{37} controls
pairing a real claim with another question's passage; a third pass adjudicated
disagreements. Agreement was \SI{99.1}{\percent} ($\kappa = \num{0.973}$) and all \num{37}
controls were rejected.

\num{179} of \num{185} claims are supported: \num{0.968} [\num{0.941}, \num{0.989}]---a
bound on this design, not a success. \num{183} of the \num{185} claims are verbatim
sentences of the passage, because the extractor copies rather than authors, so support is
near-automatic and the \emph{overstated} label never fired for want of anything
paraphrased to fire on. The failure mode this paper predicted---an over-strong claim wearing
a real quotation---does not arise at the claim layer here, and neither judge found one. All six failures are ingestion defects, not attribution errors: bare table headers,
sentences beheaded mid-clause, and two that are an author's name, institution and
email shown as medical statements, each with a citation every mechanical check passed.

The author graded \num{30} of these claims blind, stratified by the model's own label: a
census of all six failures plus \num{24} of the \num{179} the model passed. Among all six
model-flagged claims the author overturned three, having read passages the model had called
too corrupted to judge. No false-positive \emph{supported} label was observed among the
\num{24} sampled model-supported claims, although the exact lower bound on that label's
precision is \num{0.858}. Weighting the census exactly and the sample by a conservative
binomial interval gives a human rate of \num{0.984} [\num{0.846}, \num{0.984}], whose point
estimate is the largest value this two-stratum design can express.

\subsection{Does the returned claim answer the question?}
\label{sec:relevance}
Support is not usefulness. The same instrument, under a second codebook also frozen
beforehand, labelled every returned claim against the \emph{question} it was returned
for---with \num{37} controls pairing a real claim with a question from another specialty.
Two blind passes agreed \SI{97.8}{\percent} ($\kappa = \num{0.955}$) and \num{37} of
\num{37} controls were rejected.

\num{4} of the \num{185} returned claims answer their question: \num{0.022} [\num{0.005},
\num{0.045}]---a figure less stable than those decimals suggest, as below. \num{129} are \emph{partial}---on the topic but not an answer, typically a study's objective
or a textbook definition---and \num{52} are unrelated, \num{49} of them supported by the
passage they cite. At the answer level under the matched
labels, \num{5} of \num{55} answers with any claim carry one that answers and \num{12} only
unrelated ones; the URL-first arm gives \num{10} of \num{109} and \num{26}---\num{0.09} in
both.
The author then graded a stratified subsample of \num{40} blind:
agreement \SI{85}{\percent} against these labels and \SI{82.5}{\percent} against the matched
re-judging ($\kappa = \num{0.72}$, \num{0.68}), \SI{97.5}{\percent} on the \emph{relevant}
boundary under either, and a post-stratified descriptive \num{0.028}---two positive cases,
so calibration rather than population inference and no interval. Four of the six disagreements
are the author \emph{stricter} about what counts as on-topic and two the reverse; on entailment
the author was more lenient, so no single assumed bias corrects both.

Re-judging the same items under the same codebook, each run's served model recovered from its
transcript, separates two things. Entailment was judged by the same model twice, so its
unchanged \num{0.968} (agreement \SI{98.9}{\percent}, $\kappa = \num{0.83}$ on the real claims)
is repeatability, not judge-independence. Relevance crossed models and moved, \num{0.022} to
\num{0.038}: \num{11} real claims go from unrelated to \emph{partial} and \num{3} from
\emph{partial} to answering, \num{36} of \num{37} controls hold and all \num{37} still refuse
\emph{relevant}, and the adjudicator prompt differed too, accounting for at most \num{5} of the
\num{15}. That shift is \num{2.8} times the between-design difference, which is why
Section~\ref{sec:where} re-runs that comparison under one instrument. Author, first and re-run
judge give \num{0.028}, \num{0.022} and \num{0.038}.

A claim layer can therefore be almost perfectly attributed and almost never responsive.
Support and responsiveness are not substitutes, and an attribution mechanism evaluated on
the first alone is evaluated on the easier one.

\subsection{What it costs}
Table~\ref{tab:conditions} prices each check. The span gate is nearly free---\num{19}
failures in \num{351} claims---and citability withholds \num{147} of the \num{166}: retention,
shown claims over extracted, falls from \num{0.946} to \num{0.527}, and the number of answers
carrying no returned claim rises from 29 to 79 of 134. Answers, not claims, are the unit a reader feels.

The tax is not a constant. Over the 119 answerable questions, retention by question type
runs from \num{0.122} for patient-FAQ questions to \num{0.687} for guideline-comparison
questions---a factor of \num{5.6} on one system, one corpus, one set of checks. The types
differ in the evidence they need, not only in wording, so this separates kinds of question
rather than an effect of phrasing, which would need a paired-paraphrase design. What
it establishes is that a single retention figure describes no particular user.

The 15 out-of-corpus controls are reported apart, and not because they are claim-free: the
shipped configuration returned \num{25} claims on them.

\subsection{An interaction between two constraints}
Containment is a non-trivial test only on the \num{29} extracted claims whose span differs
from the claim: \num{9} failed containment, \num{18} verified and were then withheld for
citability, \num{2} were returned. The two
constraints are not independent---\SI{72.4}{\percent} of independent-span claims cite a
URL-less record against \SI{44.4}{\percent} overall. A pipeline composing attribution
constraints should report the population each one can still fail on, not only its rate.

\subsection{Where the check sits}
\label{sec:where}
The tax above is paid by withholding after generation. The alternative is to exclude the
URL-less stratum at retrieval, so a citable record takes the seat instead of the seat going
empty. We ran that design live on the same 134 questions: same enforcement code, one
environment variable, the arm recorded in every response.

Moving the constraint upstream eliminates citability-based withholding \emph{by
construction}---a claim cannot cite a record the retriever was never allowed to return. Across
the two runs we observed, returned claims rose from 185 to \num{364} and retention from
\num{0.527} to \num{0.960}, answers with no claim fell from 79 to 25, and the \num{5.6}-fold
spread across question types collapsed; extracted claims \emph{rose} too, 351 to 379, so
retrieval refilled rather than returning less---two runs of one system, not a replicated
effect. Retrieval p50 falls \num{0.8}\,s with the \num{256415}-chunk stratum out of the search
and end-to-end p50 \num{2.5}\,s, but the other \num{1.7}\,s is generation and verification
against an external API on a different day, so we claim none of it.

We found no evidence that either design changed per-claim support or responsiveness. The first
comparison crossed two judge models and two codebooks, so we re-judged the URL-first arm under
the shipped arm's codebook byte for byte, served model, adjudicator prompt and batch size.
Matched, support is \num{0.968} against \num{0.956} and responsiveness \num{0.038} against
\num{0.036}. As intervals on the \emph{difference}, by the paired-question
cluster bootstrap of Table~\ref{tab:conditions}: \num{0.012} [\num{-0.016}, \num{0.039}] and \num{0.002}
[\num{-0.020}, \num{0.027}]. Matching moved responsiveness by \num{0.008} and reversed its
sign, so the earlier pair was partly reporting the instrument; in these two runs the judge
shift of Section~\ref{sec:relevance} moves both arms alike and mostly cancels. No equivalence
margin was pre-specified, so these bound the observed difference rather than establish equivalence.
The author graded \num{40} of this arm's claims blind: agreement \SI{95}{\percent}
($\kappa = \num{0.87}$) against the original labels and \SI{92.5}{\percent}
($\kappa = \num{0.81}$) against matched labels. Both matched-model \emph{relevant} items were
graded \emph{partial} by the author; no human-relevant item was observed, leaving sparse
calibration at this boundary. The arm returns twice as many claims, including
\num{78} supported-but-unrelated ones against \num{40}. On the 15 designated out-of-corpus controls
it returns \num{41} claims against \num{25}, so refilling from citable strata also produces
confident, citable evidence for questions that have none.

Three operational facts close the section. The fail-closed verifier blocked no answer in 134
questions (55 pass, 79 revise). Median stage latencies under withholding are \num{3.3}\,s
retrieval, \num{19.7}\,s generation and \num{0.7}\,s verification, the gates not instrumented
separately, so we claim nothing about their share. And the gates run \emph{before}
verification while a \texttt{revise} verdict replaces the answer after them without re-running
them, so the absence of evidence-absent figures in delivered text is measured over all 134
answers rather than structural.

% ---------------------------------------------------------------- VII. LIMITATIONS AND CONCLUSION (<=250 words)
\section{Limitations and Conclusion}\textbf{Both judged rates are model judgements at the claim layer.} Support
(\num{0.968}) and responsiveness (\num{0.022}) come from model judges under frozen codebooks
with control arms, calibrated against the author's own blind grading of \num{30}, \num{40}
and \num{40} items across both arms---\num{110} judgements over \num{101} distinct
claims---providing sparse, arm-dependent calibration. There is no independent human rater,
and no clinician has read these answers. No judge run pinned a checkpoint; each served judge
model ID is recovered from its run
transcript and released---\texttt{claude-opus-5} for the matched re-judging---but an ID is not
a checkpoint and the interface accepts a family alias. Generator/verifier model IDs and
decoding parameters were not recorded in the captures. The narrative answer around the claims is
unenforced by policy and unjudged here.

\textbf{Scope.} One corpus, one generator, one embedding model, one language. The
attributability figures are model-extracted and span-verified; that human precision check was
never performed. The probe questions are ours, committed and hashed the day before the
first run but never registered and with no registered analysis plan, and not from an external
benchmark. The gates run before verification and a revision replaces the answer
after them, so delivered text is re-checked only by our offline audit; one answer of 134
shipped a raw verifier JSON object. The ingestion repair leaves a residual: one of 32
re-sampled chunks still interleaves, and the extractor flags 116 of 150 articles as scrambled; this flag is
unvalidated and supports no quality claim.

\textbf{Conclusion.} Traceability can be enforced by code rather than promised by a model,
and where the check sits decides what it must withhold at all: excluding uncitable records at
retrieval eliminates citability-based withholding \emph{by construction}, and across the two
runs we observed, retention rose from \num{0.527} to \num{0.960}, and returned claims from
\num{185} to \num{364}. We did not detect a difference in per-claim support or responsiveness:
under both, about \num{0.96} of returned claims are supported and \numrange{0.02}{0.04}
answer the question, with no equivalence margin pre-specified. High support coexists with low
responsiveness; \num{183} of \num{185} shipped claims are verbatim passage sentences.
Because extraction was not varied, these observations do not isolate retrieval, extraction,
and corpus answerability. Both rates belong in such reports, with explicit measurement scope.
