\begin{abstract}
Retrieval-augmented generation (RAG) is adopted for medical question answering on a promise of
traceability, yet whether a retrieval corpus's records can be cited at all, and what enforcing
citability costs at serving time, is rarely checked. We audit the corpus and serving path of
one Vietnamese medical RAG research prototype. In its index, \SI{43}{\percent} of chunks
carry no source URL, because one re-packaged dataset kept its text and dropped its links, and
a further \SI{13}{\percent} carry a URL that opens an issue index, not the article. On the
serving path, withholding uncitable claims after generation keeps \num{0.53} of extracted
claims; excluding the URL-less records at retrieval avoids that withholding by construction
(\num{0.96}, one run per design). Under both designs almost every returned attribution entry
is supported by its passage, yet only a few per hundred answer the question. Our message:
traceability of the attribution list can be enforced by code; where the citability check sits
decides what is withheld; and high support coexists with very low claim-level responsiveness.
Responsiveness is measured on returned attribution entries under a verbatim-copy extractor,
not as an end-to-end answer-quality rate, and the design comparison is two single runs.
\end{abstract}

% ---------------------------------------------------------------- I. INTRODUCTION (<=450 words)
\section{Introduction}Retrieval-augmented generation is deployed for medical question answering on a promise: the
answer is grounded in retrieved documents, and a reader who doubts it can check. In the
Vietnamese-language system we operate, \num{156} of the \num{351} claims proposed over 134
questions were uncitable before any gate: \num{154} cite a record whose corpus was re-packaged
for embedding training with its text kept and its URLs dropped, and two name no record. No
answer-quality metric we had could see this.

Mechanisms that make citations verifiable by construction are established and we claim
novelty for none of them (Section~\ref{sec:related}); they are evaluated on corpora assembled
to be cited, and attribution has been measured across languages \cite{muller2023xorattriqa}
but not, to our knowledge, on a medical corpus assembled from re-packaged data.

We therefore treat the mechanism as an instrument, not a contribution, and ask three
questions. \textbf{RQ1:} how much of a Vietnamese medical retrieval corpus can support a
citation a reader can open---stored, answering, and resolving to the cited article rather
than to an index? \textbf{RQ2:} what do span containment and stored-URL
citability cost at serving time, and how does that cost depend on where filtering is applied?
\textbf{RQ3:} does what survives enforcement---the attribution list, and the answer it
accompanies---answer the question that was asked?

That answer quality and citation quality diverge is documented
\cite{liu2023verifiability,malaviya2024expertqa,wang2025medcite}. Our contribution is a
single-system case study, and we claim novelty only for a provenance census of a medical
retrieval corpus and for how a citability check interacts with provenance loss on a serving
path. We measure, through the system's API, four quantities prior work already separates
\cite{malaviya2024expertqa}: whether a source can be traced, whether the passage supports the
claim, whether the claim answers the question, and whether the answer does.
(i) An exhaustive census of all \num{595663} indexed chunks, and a dereference audit: a
non-empty URL field is a weak proxy for an openable citation, while the article/issue shape of
the stored URL predicts the destination (\num{142} of \num{143} issue-level URLs land on an index).
(ii) A price for each check, from four nested gate configurations replayed over one captured
sweep of 134 questions, hashed and committed privately the day before the first run and
published with this paper, so they share identical generations. (iii) The same questions run
once under a second design that excludes the URL-less stratum at retrieval; we call the two
designs \emph{arms}, the \emph{shipped} arm withholding after generation (it was never
released to users) and the \emph{URL-first} arm excluding at retrieval. (iv) Two claim-level
judgement instruments with codebooks frozen
before use and control arms; after submission, adapted versions judged withheld claims and the
narrative answers, which we report as exploratory. Evaluation code, questions, labels, judging
prompts, redacted run records and the post-submission analyses are released at
\url{https://github.com/jprosun/vi-medrag-audit}.

\section{Related Work}\label{sec:related}
\textbf{A citation is a promise, not a property.} Generative search engines produce citations
that often fail to support the sentence they are attached to \cite{liu2023verifiability};
hence benchmarks for citation quality \cite{gao2023alce} and automatic attribution scoring
\cite{yue2023attrscore}; deep-research agents resolve above \SI{94}{\percent} of links while
being factually accurate on \SIrange{39}{77}{\percent} \cite{onweller2026citednotverified}, so
a citation that resolves is not one that supports, and a correct citation need not be what the
model used \cite{wallat2025correctness}. Nor is a supported claim a responsive one:
answer coverage has been scored by decomposing the query into sub-questions
\cite{xie2025subquestion}; we judge each returned claim singly against the whole question.

\textbf{Verifiable by construction.} Requiring each statement to carry a verbatim quote is
established: GopherCite answers with a supporting quote and abstains when unconfident
\cite{menick2022gophercite}; later work prompts or aligns models to quote
\cite{weller2024according,zhang2024quotetuning}, locates spans post hoc
\cite{yeginbergen2026fullcite}, verifies each claim before release \cite{qian2025vericite},
certifies medical claims selectively \cite{kan2026claimselective}, and buffers streamed tokens
until a guard clears them \cite{sentguard2026}. That enforcement withholds content is known
too: conformal methods trade coverage for factuality \cite{mohri2024conformal}, and citation
correctness trades against coverage on English benchmarks \cite{saxena2025coverage}. Ours are
the simplest forms of these, used as an instrument; what they meet on a serving path over a
corpus never assembled to be cited is unreported.

\textbf{Medical citation audits.} ExpertQA had experts rate each claim separately for
attribution, informativeness to the question and source reliability
\cite{malaviya2024expertqa}; search engines that copy from cited pages pair high citation
precision with low utility ($r=-0.96$) \cite{liu2023verifiability}. In medicine, adding
retrieval raised one model's answer accuracy while lowering its citation recall and precision
\cite{wang2025medcite}; an automated audit found \SIrange{50}{90}{\percent} of responses not
fully supported and, without web access, valid URLs only \SIrange{40}{70}{\percent} of the
time \cite{wu2025sourcecheckup}; sentence-level checks support only \SIrange{27}{41}{\percent}
of PubMed-grounded citation pairs \cite{ma2026vericite}; expert review finds
\SI{22}{\percent} of top-16 retrieved passages relevant \cite{kim2025rethinkingmedrag}. These
evaluate inline citations under other criteria: context for Section~\ref{sec:cost}, not
comparison points.

\textbf{Vietnamese resources.} Medical RAG is benchmarked for answer quality
\cite{xiong2024mirage}; Vietnamese medical NLP has question-answering datasets
\cite{tran2024vimedaqa}, benchmarks \cite{vm14k2025} and graph-grounded regulatory QA
\cite{vihermes2026}, none auditing whether a retrieval corpus's records can be cited.
Metadata loss on hosting platforms is documented: an audit of over 1{,}800 text datasets
found licences omitted for more than \SI{70}{\percent} \cite{longpre2024provenance}.

\section{System and Enforcement as Instrument}\label{sec:system}\subsection{Definitions}
The system answers Vietnamese medical questions over a Qdrant index of \num{595663} chunks
embedded with \texttt{bge-m3}, combining dense retrieval with an article-level lexical
index. Each answer path emits, per proposed claim, a triple: the \emph{claim}, a
\emph{supporting span}, and the \emph{chunk} the span is drawn from. Every check below is
defined on that triple.

A router picks a retrieval \emph{profile} per question, identically in both arms: \num{67}
questions ran the \emph{standard} profile, which pools fewer chunks (top-$k$ \num{12}) and
forms claims with a rule-based selector that copies query-focused sentences from the
top-ranked chunks verbatim as both claim and span, and \num{67} the \emph{deep} profile
(top-$k$ \num{20}), whose LLM extractor may paraphrase. The standard profile produced
\num{208} of the \num{351} claims. The selector's source is not released. Profile is set
almost by question type, so the two are confounded. A mode multiplier scales the base pool,
so answers carry \numrange{22}{57} retrieved chunks, \num{4024} in all.

A span is \emph{located} when it occurs as an exact substring of the chunk it names after a
normalisation documented in the released code (NFC, header removal, lowercasing, Vietnamese
number separators, dash, quote and whitespace variants; diacritics are not folded, since
Vietnamese words can differ by tone alone; spans under 12 characters are rejected).
A record is \emph{citable} when it carries a non-empty source URL; we also report whether that
URL is article- or issue-level (Table~\ref{tab:census}). A claim is \emph{returned} when it
passes every gate and appears in the machine-readable attribution list sent with the answer;
a located claim need not be returned.

A \emph{claim figure} is a percentage, a decimal, or an integer of at least three digits,
citation markers removed and bare four-digit values in 1900--2099 excluded as years unless
digit-grouped. A figure is \emph{evidence-absent} when it appears in no located span \emph{and} nowhere in
the retrieved evidence---lexical absence, not a finding that the value is wrong---and
\emph{unattributed} when it appears in that evidence but inside no located span.

\subsection{Gates}
Figure~\ref{fig:pipeline} shows the path. Four checks run in code, in this order, before the
answer is verified. Every withheld claim records a \texttt{drop\_reason}, a code naming the
gate that withheld it, which is what makes the offline replay of Table~\ref{tab:conditions}
possible.

\textbf{Span gate.} A claim with no span, an unresolvable chunk identifier, or a span not
contained in its chunk is withheld.

\textbf{Citability gate.} A claim whose span was located but whose record carries no source
URL is withheld; the chunk still reaches the generator as context, and located-ness and
returned-ness are counted separately.

\textbf{Numeric gate.} Statements carrying an evidence-absent figure are removed and a visible note
says what went and why; unattributed figures are counted and left.

\textbf{Verifier, fail-closed.} The mechanical findings go \emph{inside} the verifier's
prompt, it may not return \texttt{pass} while any of them stands, and every path that could
return without a clearance (no client, an exception, an unusable status, a \texttt{pass} over
a standing finding) fails closed to \texttt{revise}.

\textbf{Buffer-then-commit.} Tokens are held until verification returns.

\subsection{What the guarantee is and is not}
Span containment proves that the cited string \emph{exists} in the cited chunk, not that it
\emph{supports} the claim, which Section~\ref{sec:cost} measures. The rate of returned claims
whose span verifies is therefore 1.00 \emph{by construction}, a design invariant rather than a
result, and nearly vacuous: \num{183} of the \num{185} returned claims have a span identical
to the claim.

Three scope statements follow. ``Citation'' means an entry of the returned attribution list;
the interface does not yet render attribution cards, so every count below is over API
responses, and we say \emph{returned} rather than displayed. The answers also carry inline
\texttt{[n]} references to context chunks (133 of 134); those are not evaluated here. The
narrative answer runs under a hybrid policy permitting uncited background knowledge, and
enforcement covers figures as defined above: one- and two-digit integers are outside it, and
\num{534} of them with units (doses, ages, durations) occur in 98 answers.

\section{Corpus Attributability Audit}\label{sec:audit}
We answer RQ1 by counting, not sampling: one payload-only scroll of all \num{595663} points of
the live collection, checked against the collection count (Table~\ref{tab:census}).

\textbf{Two fifths of the index cannot be cited at all.} \num{256415} chunks,
\SI{43.05}{\percent}, carry no source URL. They are a single stratum, a re-packaging of the
Long Ch\^au pharmacy chain's patient-education pages published on Hugging Face as
\path{mtue29/vietnamese-medical-dataset}, which we ingested with its text and without its
links; its Apache-2.0 tag is the re-packager's, and the source site's terms were not checked. The chunks are still
retrieved as context but cannot carry a citation: one ingestion decision of ours, analogous
to the licence-metadata loss documented by \cite{longpre2024provenance}; we make no claim
that the share generalises. The loss is recoverable: matching the 52 titles behind the 154 URL-less claims to
the source site's public sitemap (\texttt{robots.txt} honoured) recovered 50 pages whose
heading matches the title, and for 133 of the 147 claims later withheld for citability the
span occurs verbatim on the recovered page.

\textbf{The untraceable stratum is over-represented among retrieved chunks, not among
claims.} Over the 134 questions it supplied \num{2472} of \num{4024} retrieved chunks,
\SI{61.4}{\percent} [\num{0.569}, \num{0.660}], against \SI{43.0}{\percent} of the index, a
lift of \num{1.43};
the fourteen English-language strata fell from \SI{18.8}{\percent} of the index to
\SI{0.9}{\percent} of what was retrieved. Among Vietnamese-language chunks the stratum is
\SI{54.4}{\percent} of the index and \SI{63.1}{\percent} of what was retrieved, a lift of
\num{1.16} [\num{1.07}, \num{1.24}]. The share follows the question type---\num{0.886} for
patient-FAQ questions against \num{0.431} for study-result questions; type is confounded with
profile, so the data cannot separate grid from retriever. Every retrieved chunk lacking a source URL came from it, and it
supplied \num{154} of the \num{156} uncitable claims; the other two cite \emph{nothing}, an
empty chunk identifier withheld by the span gate. At the claim level, however, its share is
\num{154} of \num{351}, \SI{43.9}{\percent}, essentially its index share: the
over-representation describes the retrieved pool, not what the extractor selected from it.
The system's own metadata puts
this stratum in its lowest trust tier; the highest (WHO, Ministry of Health, NCI) supplied
\SI{1.8}{\percent} of retrieved chunks.

\textbf{A non-empty URL is not one a reader can open.} A further \num{79678} chunks,
\SI{13.4}{\percent}, from four of the five Vietnamese journal strata carry only an
\emph{issue}-level URL. Their \num{143} distinct URLs are a census: \num{142} answer and land
on an issue index, a table of contents standing for about \num{557} chunks each. The same
pattern classifies stored and final URLs, so the
stored URL's shape predicts the destination; only the binary non-empty test is weak.
Dereferencing honoured \texttt{robots.txt}, which refused five hosts covering \num{24209}
chunks; we probed \num{367} URLs, \num{289} frozen before any request (the census included)
and \num{78} added adaptively. Failures clustered by host: three NIH hosts failed on all
\num{35} probes (27 HTTP 404, 8 unreachable), hosts holding \num{2880} URLs behind
\num{16045} chunks in one English stratum. Chunk-weighted reachability is \num{0.955} on the
frozen sample (Bonferroni joint interval across hosts \numrange{0.69}{0.97}) and \num{0.949}
(\numrange{0.72}{0.97}) with the adaptive probes; a confirmatory probability-proportional
sample of never-probed URLs gives \num{0.93} (per-host envelope \numrange{0.65}{0.99}), an
author's blind labelling of \num{40} reached pages agreed with the destination test on
\num{39}, and no corpus-level composite is reported because its unbiased weighted estimator
exceeds one. As point figures under host-level exclusion, the stored
not-issue share is \num{0.436}, \num{0.409} without the three failing hosts and \num{0.368}
without the five refused ones; at serving time only \SI{27.5}{\percent} of retrieved chunks
carried a non-issue URL. For what is \emph{returned}, on 2026-10-11 we dereferenced the
\num{115} distinct URLs cited in either arm, fetching \num{113} (two robots-refused), and
matched each page's \texttt{citation\_title} to the cited record, since the journal
platform's page titles are generic. Of returned claims,
\num{0.903} [\num{0.818}, \num{0.972}] shipped and \num{0.777} [\num{0.693}, \num{0.855}]
URL-first open on the cited article; the rest land on an issue index (17 and 71), a page
without article metadata (1 and 4) or a refused host (0 and 6); none fetched was dead.
Counting only article matches, retention is \num{0.476} and \num{0.747}, not \num{0.527}
and \num{0.960}: the URL-first arm refills partly from issue-level strata.

% ---------------------------------------------------------------- V. ATTRIBUTION GAP AND COST OF ENFORCEMENT (<=700 words, Tables III--IV)
\section{Attribution Gap and the Cost of Enforcement}\label{sec:cost}
All results below are on 134 questions written to a specialty $\times$ question-type grid by
13 blind \texttt{claude-opus-5} agents (no corpus, web or tool access; 15 out-of-corpus
controls), edited after three same-model auditors (18 edits, one drop of 64 findings),
then hashed and committed privately the day before the first run and published with this
paper. The patient-FAQ questions are therefore simulated consumer phrasing. The four gate
configurations, C0 (no gate) to C3 (span, numeric and citability gates together), are
replayed offline from one captured sweep, so they share identical generations; they are
configurations of one serving path, while Section~\ref{sec:where} compares the two arms, each
run end to end once.

\subsection{What enforcement changes in the returned attribution list}
Of 351 extracted claims, \num{156} are uncitable---\num{154} citing a
record with no source URL, two citing no record at all; \num{67} figures appear in no
retrieved evidence at all, in a quarter of answers
(\num{0.254}, 95\,\% CI [\num{0.187}, \num{0.328}]). Enforcement withholds every citation to a
URL-less record by construction and removes the 58 statements that carried an
evidence-absent figure; no delivered answer retains one of that class.

The gap it does \emph{not} close is larger than the one it does. Of \num{708} figures in the
delivered answers, \num{157} (\SI{22.2}{\percent}) appear among a located span's numbers, by
numeric token only; the remaining \num{551}, \SI{77.8}{\percent}, are in the retrieved
evidence but pinned to nothing. A system can satisfy ``every returned claim is
span-verified'' and still deliver most of its quantities unattributed.

\subsection{Does the citation support the claim?}
Containment is not support, so we measured support. Two blind passes of one served model,
\texttt{claude-opus-5}, labelled all \num{185} returned claims against the passage each
cites---each pass without sight of the other or of the system's verdicts, under a codebook
frozen beforehand, with \num{37} controls pairing a real claim with another question's
passage; a third pass adjudicated disagreements. The passes agreed on \SI{99.1}{\percent}
($\kappa = \num{0.973}$) of all \num{222} items, a within-model repeatability figure that the
\num{37} easy-negative controls (all rejected) inflate; on the \num{185} real claims it is
\SI{98.9}{\percent} ($\kappa = \num{0.85}$). There was no second, independent judge.

\num{179} of \num{185} claims are supported: \num{0.968} [\num{0.941}, \num{0.989}]---a
bound on this design, not a success. \num{183} of the \num{185} claims are verbatim
sentences of the passage, because the extractor copies rather than authors, so support is
near-automatic and the \emph{overstated} label never fired for want of anything
paraphrased to fire on. All six failures are ingestion defects, not attribution errors: bare
table headers, sentences beheaded mid-clause, and an author's name, institution and email
shown as medical statements.

The author graded \num{30} of these claims blind, stratified by the model's label: all six
failures, of which the author overturned three, and \num{24} of the \num{179} passed, none
overturned (exact lower bound on that label's precision \num{0.858}). Weighting the census
exactly and the sample conservatively gives a human rate of \num{0.984} [\num{0.846},
\num{0.984}], the largest value this two-stratum design can express.

\subsection{Does the returned claim answer the question?}
\label{sec:relevance}
Support is not usefulness. Two blind passes of a second served model,
\texttt{claude-fable-5-1}, under a second codebook also frozen beforehand, labelled every
returned claim against the \emph{question} it was returned for---with \num{37} controls
pairing a real claim with a question from another specialty. The passes agreed on
\SI{97.8}{\percent} ($\kappa = \num{0.955}$) of \num{222} items and \SI{97.3}{\percent}
($\kappa = \num{0.94}$) of the real claims, again repeatability; \num{37} of \num{37}
controls were rejected. This shipped-arm codebook also told the judge that support was
``already established at \SI{96.8}{\percent}'' of cases, a numeric prior carried over from
the entailment instrument and present in this arm's codebook only; the matched re-judging of
Section~\ref{sec:where} reused that codebook byte for byte, so under the matched labels the
prior is shared by both arms rather than removed.

\num{4} of the \num{185} returned claims answer their question: \num{0.022} [\num{0.008},
\num{0.054}] (Wilson)---less stable than those decimals suggest, as below. \num{129} are
\emph{partial}---on the topic but not an answer, typically a study's objective
or a textbook definition---and \num{52} are unrelated, \num{49} of them supported by the
passage they cite. The author graded \num{40} blind (agreement \SI{85}{\percent},
$\kappa = \num{0.72}$; post-stratified \num{0.028} from two positives, so calibration, not
inference). Re-judging with the served model ID fixed (\texttt{claude-opus-5} for both
instruments) kept support at \num{0.968} (same model: repeatability) and moved responsiveness
to \num{0.038}, a judge shift \num{2.8} times the original between-design difference
(URL-first: \num{0.027}), which is why Section~\ref{sec:where} compares designs under one
instrument. This is a claim-layer observation: the unit is one returned attribution entry,
judged singly against the question, under an extractor that copies passage sentences
verbatim; it is not an end-to-end answer-quality rate.

\textbf{The answers answer; the attribution list does not (exploratory).} After submission
we judged every narrative answer and every extracted claim in both arms, withheld ones
included, with an adapted instrument: passage hidden, support prior removed, the answer as
unit for answers, and disputes adjudicated by pass B (\texttt{claude-opus-5-5}). On claims
pass A (\texttt{claude-fable-5-1}) and pass B agreed at $\kappa = \num{0.90}$; on answers
$\kappa = \num{0.98}$, with 218 of 268 pairs pass-B on both passes, so a third pass by
\texttt{claude-sonnet-5-5} was added ($\kappa = \num{0.93}$ against the final labels). No control was labelled responsive by either model: \num{56} cross-specialty
pairings and \num{40} same-specialty siblings pairing a question with another's
answer. No human graded answers, and the label ignores correctness.

On the 119 answerable questions \num{0.773} of shipped and \num{0.815} of URL-first answers
answer the question (\num{0.782} and \num{0.765} under the third judge), against
\numrange{0.011}{0.022} and \numrange{0.011}{0.027} of returned claims across passes (final 2
of 185 [\num{0.003}, \num{0.039}] and 4 of 364 [\num{0.004}, \num{0.028}], Wilson); the drop
from the original instrument's 4 to 2 follows the judge model (pass A 4, pass B 2). Per
answer, 2 shipped answers carry a responsive returned claim, and 42 of the 55 with a non-empty
list answer without one (URL-first 77 of 109). The gap is partly built in---one verbatim
sentence rarely answers an explanatory question, and \numrange{0.69}{0.72} of returned claims
are on topic---and it is not uniform: study-result questions are answered 3 of 24 times, the
other types 18 to 24 of 24; the profile and coverage-mode splits (deep 57 of 61 against
standard 35 of 58; system-reported strong evidence 9 of 19 against background knowledge 27
of 30) are confounded with that type effect. The 15 out-of-corpus controls, meant to test
abstention, were answered responsively 9 times (URL-first 7) and labelled unanswerable once,
in URL-first.

Responsive claims were few: 13 of 351 extracted (deep profile 11 of 143, standard 2 of 208),
and citability withheld 11, from 5 of the 7 questions that had any (10 distinct sentences):
patient-education sentences cited for patient questions, whose support was not judged.
Withheld minus returned, the responsive rate differs by \num{0.064} [\num{-0.002},
\num{0.146}] and the on-topic rate by \num{-0.22} [\num{-0.40}, \num{-0.04}], and the withheld
claims come from the lowest trust tier. With the recovered links (Section~\ref{sec:audit}), 10 of the 11 and
133 of the 147 withheld claims would have been citable, and C3 retention would be \num{0.906}
rather than \num{0.527}. Excluding the stratum at retrieval moves the loss upstream, where
retention cannot see it: URL-first extracted 4 responsive claims of 379 (patient-FAQ 1 of
71), against 13 of 351 (10 of 57), one run per design. Under verbatim-copy extraction, support and responsiveness
are not substitutes, and an attribution mechanism evaluated on the first alone is evaluated on
the easier one.

\subsection{What it costs}
Table~\ref{tab:conditions} prices each check. The span gate is nearly free---\num{19}
failures in \num{351} claims---and citability withholds \num{147} of the \num{166}: retention,
shown claims over extracted, falls from \num{0.946} to \num{0.527}, and the number of answers
carrying no returned claim rises from 29 to 79 of 134.

The tax is not a constant. Over the 119 answerable questions (23--24 per type), retention by
question type runs from \num{0.122} [\num{0.000}, \num{0.313}] for patient-FAQ questions to
\num{0.687} [\num{0.487}, \num{0.854}] for guideline-comparison questions---a factor of
\num{5.6} on one system, one corpus, one set of checks, with disjoint intervals. Profile and
type are confounded (Section~\ref{sec:system}), and separating question kind from phrasing
would need a paired-paraphrase design; what it establishes is that a single retention figure
describes no particular user.

\subsection{Where the check sits}
\label{sec:where}
The alternative to withholding after generation is to exclude the URL-less stratum at
retrieval, so a citable record takes the seat. We ran it live on the same 134 questions with
the same enforcement code and one environment variable, but on a later build
(\texttt{47536e9}) and another day; the shipped sweep also spans one server restart.

This eliminates citability-based withholding \emph{by construction}. Across the two runs,
returned claims rose from 185 to \num{364}, retention from \num{0.527} to \num{0.960}
[\num{0.931}, \num{0.984}], answers with no claim fell from 79 to 25 (paired difference
\num{-0.40} [\num{-0.49}, \num{-0.31}]), and the spread across types fell to \num{1.4}-fold;
extracted claims rose too, 351 to 379, so retrieval refilled. Only the first follows by
construction; the rest are one run per design, not a replicated effect.

We found no evidence that either design changed per-claim support or responsiveness. The
URL-first arm's original labels came from \texttt{claude-fable-5-1} on both instruments under
a shorter codebook, so the unmatched support contrast crossed models. Re-judged under the
shipped arm's codebook, served model (\texttt{claude-opus-5}), adjudicator prompt and batch
size, support is \num{0.968} against \num{0.956} and responsiveness \num{0.038} against \num{0.036}
(shipped minus URL-first, paired-question cluster bootstrap: \num{0.012} [\num{-0.016},
\num{0.039}] and \num{0.002} [\num{-0.020}, \num{0.027}]); matching reversed the sign of the
responsiveness difference, so the unmatched pair partly reported the instrument. No
equivalence margin was pre-specified, and the smallest responsiveness difference detectable at
\SI{80}{\percent} power is \num{0.034}, about the base rate. The author graded \num{40} of this
arm's claims blind (agreement \SI{92.5}{\percent}, $\kappa = \num{0.81}$). The arm returns
twice as many claims, including \num{78} supported-but-unrelated ones against \num{40}, the
same \SI{21}{\percent} in both arms, and \num{41} claims on the 15 out-of-corpus controls
against \num{25}.

The verifier's verdicts are mostly code: of the
shipped arm's 79 \texttt{revise}, 66 override a model \texttt{pass} over a standing finding,
10 recover a truncated reply and 3 are the model's own (URL-first: 38, 7, none, plus one
block that still returned 3 claims), so the verdict mostly records that a gate fired. One
shipped answer delivered a raw block verdict as its text.
The gates run \emph{before} verification and are not re-run on the delivered text, so the
absence of evidence-absent figures in it is measured over all 134 answers, not structural.

% ---------------------------------------------------------------- VII. LIMITATIONS AND CONCLUSION (<=250 words)
\section{Limitations and Conclusion}\textbf{Every judged rate is a model judgement.} Support
(\num{0.968}) and claim responsiveness (\num{0.022}) come from model judges under frozen
codebooks with control arms, calibrated against the author's blind grading of \num{110} items
over \num{101} distinct claims across both arms: sparse, arm-dependent calibration. The
answer-level and withheld-claim rates come from instruments adapted after the data were seen,
are blind to correctness, and have no human calibration. The only human rater is the author,
who also built the system and wrote both codebooks; no independent rater or clinician has
read these answers. Served judge model IDs are recovered from transcripts and released, but an
ID is not a checkpoint; generator and verifier IDs and decoding parameters were not recorded.

\textbf{Scope.} One corpus, one generator, one embedding model, one language, and
model-authored questions committed privately the day before the first run; nothing was
registered. The per-type split, the URL-first arm, the adaptive URL probes, the matched
re-judging, the answer-level and withheld-claim judging, the URL recovery and the dereference
of returned URLs were added after the first results. The generator ran once per design, on
different builds. No oracle check tested whether a retrieved chunk could answer each question,
the support of withheld claims was not judged, the attributability figures have no human
precision check, and delivered text is re-checked only by our offline audit.

\textbf{Conclusion.} On this system, traceability of the attribution list can be enforced by
code rather than promised by a model, and where the check sits decides what it must withhold:
excluding uncitable records at retrieval eliminates citability-based withholding \emph{by
construction} (retention \num{0.527} to \num{0.960}, \num{0.476} to \num{0.747} counting only
citations that open on the article) but moves the loss of responsive content upstream, where
retention cannot see it; restoring links at ingestion would have kept 133 of the 147 withheld
claims citable. Under both designs about \num{0.96} of returned claims are supported by their
passage---near-automatically, since 183 of 185 are verbatim copies---and \numrange{0.01}{0.04}
answer the question. That is a claim-layer observation on returned attribution entries under
a verbatim-copy extractor on one corpus, not an end-to-end answer-quality rate; in exploratory
judging most answers to answerable questions do answer. We did not detect a design difference
in support or responsiveness, but the comparison is two single runs and underpowered.
Support, claim responsiveness and answer responsiveness belong in such reports separately.
